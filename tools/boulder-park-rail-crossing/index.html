Boulder Park Dr × the railroad — TEST▾

A test scene, same treatment as the crest and Tatum ones — package terrain, real individual trees, look-test road — framed on the railroad crossing on Boulder Park Dr, east of Fairburn Rd. Test scene only, not game data; the package itself is untouched (the cut and fill are worked into this scene's copy of the ground).

The crossing: the CSX Atlanta Terminal Subdivision, single track, crossing Boulder Park Dr at grade at 33.74983, −84.50144 — about 440 m east of Fairburn Rd and 430 m west of the I-285 bridge. From here the line runs south-west past the Mays and Fairburn crossings toward the yard side — the tracks that pass behind the Dollar Mill neighbourhood. Rail top at the crossing: 877.5 ft.

The railroad holds its own grade. Across this whole frame the rails fall about 21 ft on one steady slope, while the natural ground rolls through a 14 ft range of its own. North-west of the crossing the ground dips away and the line rides an embankment — up to 4.1 ft of fill under the ballast. South-east, the ground rises and the line passes through a cut up to 10.6 ft deep. The ground is cut and filled to meet the railroad — the rails never follow the ground's bumps, never float, never bury.

The road rises to meet the rails. Boulder Park Dr's profile flattens onto the rail-top level (its natural crest at the tracks, 879 ft, is trimmed about half a metre); the rails and ties run continuous through a crossing panel set in the road surface — no structure, the road simply goes over the tracks. A crossbuck and flashing-light signal stands on each approach (watch them flash).

The trees are real trees — 570 of them, placed one by one from the package canopy data, same as the earlier scenes: mature hardwoods 12–20 m, foliage starting about 8 ft up, just over the 6′1″ player's head.

Clearings — checked against the actual tree list: 0 trees in the road corridor, 0 trees in the rail corridor, and the crossing's sight lines are kept clear — you can see down the tracks from the driver's seat.
